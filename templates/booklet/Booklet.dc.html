Why this matters

What happens when disaster does not only destroy homes, but also interrupts livelihoods, knowledge, identity and opportunity?

Three shocks in six years — COVID-19, Cyclone Seroja and the 2026 earthquake — hit the same communities in East Nusa Tenggara. Each time, recovery funding arrived for buildings. What was interrupted underneath stayed interrupted.

Livelihood interrupted

Income from land and loom stops while the cost of living carries on.

Knowledge transfer interrupted

Teaching moves to survival. What elders hold is not passed on during a shock.

Identity and opportunity interrupted

People are left to rebuild as beneficiaries instead of as owners of their own capacity.

These communities are not short of intelligence, skill, relationships or assets02
The big idea

Tenun is the entry point, not the destination.

A length of cloth carries a record: which cotton, whose hands, which motif, how long on the loom, what the community allows to be told. That record is intelligence — held, taught and refined over generations. Beyond Tenun treats it as intelligence worth investing in, and builds the pathway that lets communities use it, be paid for it, record it and teach it.

The three-year arc

Restore. Regenerate. Flourish.

Year 1
Restore

Go to the ground first. Assessment across the six sites, validation with each community, then activities, roles, timeline and resources agreed together. Seed and Loom work begins: land and cotton restored, weaving practice strengthened.

Year 2
Regenerate

Trace and Teach come online: the weaver's record attached to the cloth, teacher capacity built, a locally rooted curriculum co-created with the communities and local education authorities.

Year 3
Flourish

The Local Impact Hub operates as a place, not a program: capacity building, incubation and enterprise development run by people from the communities themselves.
